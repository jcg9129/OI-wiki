## OI Wiki is not a place to publish original research

As a wiki, **OI Wiki** is not a place to publish [original research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research) (such as **new theories and solutions**, **original viewpoints**, or **self-coined definitions or terms**). For example:

-   If you discover an unconventional approach to a problem and cannot show that this approach has already been applied to other problems, you **should not** create a separate page for it on **OI Wiki**.
-   If you propose a new algorithm or data structure and cannot show that it has already been used to solve some class of problems in competitive programming, you **should not** submit it to **OI Wiki**.

## OI Wiki is not a news aggregator

As a knowledge hub focused on competitive-programming content in a primarily Chinese-language context, **OI Wiki** emphasizes information that is **stable, well-established, and widely recognized**.

In other words, unless the information is released by an authoritative body (such as a contest-format update announced by the China Computer Federation), the content you contribute should be tried and tested, widely recognized, and unlikely to become outdated within a reasonable time frame. For example:

-   If you find that Dr. X has published a new algorithm somewhere and you want to add it to **OI Wiki**, you should first observe whether it becomes widely recognized (e.g. usable as a generally strong approach in formal contests) and whether it is sufficiently distinct from algorithms already on **OI Wiki**.
-   If you notice controversy surrounding some software or person mentioned on **OI Wiki**, please follow the relevant announcements in the comments and elsewhere, and do not repeatedly open issues to raise the matter. The purpose of **OI Wiki** is to record **information that endures**, not **time-sensitive, transient information**.

## OI Wiki is not an archive

The main **OI Wiki** site does not collect materials such as **papers**, **slides**, **lecture notes**, or **manuals**. If you would like to submit competitive-programming materials, please head to [OI-wiki/libs](https://github.com/OI-wiki/libs) instead.

## OI Wiki is not a promotional tool

**OI Wiki** is a knowledge hub. It is **not** a soapbox, a forum, or a promotional tool. Therefore, when adding or modifying entries, please do not:

-   Express opinions or commentary: the content of **OI Wiki** must be **objective and neutral**. If you want to share opinions or commentary about **OI Wiki**, please head to the comments section or the issue page. If your opinions or commentary are unrelated to **OI Wiki**, please head to a personal blog or forum instead.
-   Engage in promotion of any kind: for the purpose of documenting knowledge, **OI Wiki** may accept descriptions of "websites or software already **widely known** in the field of competitive programming". Beyond that, **OI Wiki** will not accept any promotional content from anyone other than sponsors.

## OI Wiki is not an authority

As a community project that relies mainly on user contributions, **OI Wiki** is not authoritative, and should not and cannot serve as an authoritative body. **OI Wiki** can be used as a reference for learning competitive-programming knowledge, but not as a standard textbook. As a community-maintained reference site, you should not treat **OI Wiki** as a final authoritative standard (such as a "syllabus" for competitive programming), nor should you blindly trust all of its content. **OI Wiki** is not responsible for any consequences arising from the use of its content.

## OI Wiki is not a personal blog

Based on the word "wiki", **OI Wiki** is at its core a community that relies on users **collaboratively editing content**. This means the content you provide should **minimize personal flavor as much as possible**, serve the wiki as a whole, and pay attention to the structure of the entries you contribute to. Do not approach wiki entries with the mindset of writing a personal blog post. For example:

-   You **should not** use **OI Wiki** as a navigation hub for your personal blog.
-   You **should not** add irrelevant content such as an entry's history, personal gripes, or corny jokes to its description.

The examples above reflect common mindsets when writing a personal blog, and such things are unwelcome on **OI Wiki**, which advocates objectivity and neutrality.

## OI Wiki is not a translation-standards committee

The target audience of **OI Wiki** is not historians or linguists. Therefore, when translating foreign-language terms, **OI Wiki** prefers translations that have **already gained wide acceptance** in a Chinese-language context, even if they may be flawed.

When a translation is already widely known and accepted, blindly changing it in the name of "correctness" only causes greater confusion and disorder, and the wordplay it triggers hurts communication efficiency.

## OI Wiki is not an encyclopedia

The content collected on **OI Wiki** should be limited to the parts of computer science, mathematics, and similar fields that **have already been applied in competitive programming**. Other fields or knowledge unrelated to competitive programming are not suitable for inclusion on **OI Wiki**.

For example, the following fields or topics are **not suitable** for inclusion on **OI Wiki**:

-   Unrelated fields such as history and art;
-   Knowledge that belongs to a relevant field but currently cannot be applied in competitive programming, such as the [TBN matrix](https://learnopengl.com/Advanced-Lighting/Normal-Mapping);
-   Domain algorithms that currently cannot be applied in competitive programming, such as [PID control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PID_controller) and the [finite element method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_element_method);
-   General algorithms that currently cannot be applied in competitive programming, such as [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning) and [reinforcement learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning).

## OI Wiki is not documentation or a learning guide for programming languages

Compared with code implementations, the algorithms collected on **OI Wiki** should be more concerned with the algorithms themselves. A concrete implementation of an algorithm serves only as a more detailed aid to understanding or as an implementation hint, not as a convenience for learners of a specific language. If you want to learn a particular language, you should read that language's official documentation and similar resources.

For the purpose of documenting knowledge, **OI Wiki** may include simple usage guides for languages commonly used in competitive programming. Beyond that, **OI Wiki** will not include content with little relation to competitive programming and algorithms, such as "the implementation details of some function in some standard library of some programming language".
