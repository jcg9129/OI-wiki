This article specifies the recommended notation for mathematical symbols in **OI Wiki** and gives some usage examples.

This article is revised with reference to the symbol tables of [GB/T 3102.11-1993](https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/gb/newGbInfo?hcno=3DE79450D562E62D41CB6E79FF411054), [ISO 80000-2:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/64973.html), and *Concrete Mathematics*, so it is largely compatible with the symbol system of textbooks commonly used in China and with the customary symbol system in OI contexts.

For the LaTeX notation of the symbols, please refer to [the source code of this article](https://github.com/OI-wiki/OI-wiki/blob/master/docs/intro/symbol.md?plain=1).

## Mathematical logic

| No.                   | Symbol, expression        | Meaning, equivalent expression         | Notes and examples                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a id="n1.1"></a>n1.1 | $p \land q$               | conjunction of $p$ and $q$             | $p$ and $q$.                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| <a id="n1.2"></a>n1.2 | $p \lor q$                | disjunction of $p$ and $q$             | $p$ or $q$;<br>here "or" is inclusive, i.e. if at least one of $p$, $q$ is a true statement, then $p \lor q$ is true.                                                                                    |
| <a id="n1.3"></a>n1.3 | $\lnot p$                 | negation of $p$                        | not $p$.                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| <a id="n1.4"></a>n1.4 | $p \implies q$            | $p$ implies $q$;<br>if $p$ is true, then $q$ is true | $q \impliedby p$ and $p \implies q$ are synonymous.                                                                                                                                        |
| <a id="n1.5"></a>n1.5 | $p \iff q$                | $p$ is equivalent to $q$               | $(p \implies q) \land (q \implies p)$ and $p \iff q$ are synonymous.                                                                                                                                    |
| <a id="n1.6"></a>n1.6 | $(\forall~x \in A)~~p(x)$ | for all $x$ in $A$, the proposition $p(x)$ is true | If it is clear from context which set $A$ is being considered, the notation $(\forall~x)~~p(x)$ may be used.<br>$\forall$ is called the universal quantifier.<br>For the meaning of $x \in A$, see [n2.1](#n2.1). |
| <a id="n1.7"></a>n1.7 | $(\exists~x \in A)~~p(x)$ | there exists an $x$ belonging to $A$ such that $p(x)$ is true | If it is clear from context which set $A$ is being considered, the notation $(\exists~x)~~p(x)$ may be used.<br>$\exists$ is called the existential quantifier.<br>For the meaning of $x \in A$, see [n2.1](#n2.1).<br>$(\exists!~x)~~p(x)$ (the uniqueness quantifier) denotes that there is exactly one $x$ such that $p(x)$ is true.<br>$\exists!$ can also be written $\exists^1$. |

## Set theory

| No.                     | Symbol, expression                                    | Meaning, equivalent expression                | Notes and examples                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a id="n2.1"></a>n2.1   | $x \in A$                                             | $x$ belongs to $A$, $x$ is an element of the set $A$ | $A \ni x$ and $x \in A$ are synonymous.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| <a id="n2.2"></a>n2.2   | $y \notin A$                                          | $y$ does not belong to $A$, $y$ is not an element of the set $A$ |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| <a id="n2.3"></a>n2.3   | $\{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\}$                            | the set containing the elements $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ | It can also be written $\{x_i ~\vert~ i \in I\}$, where $I$ denotes the index set.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| <a id="n2.4"></a>n2.4   | $\{x \in A ~\vert~ p(x)\}$                            | the set of all elements of $A$ for which the proposition $p(x)$ is true | For example $\{x \in \textbf{R} ~\vert~ x \geq 5\}$;<br>if it is clear from context which set $A$ is being considered, the notation $\{x ~\vert~ p(x)\}$ may be used (e.g. $\{x ~\vert~ x \geq 5\}$ when only the reals are considered).<br>$\vert$ may also be replaced by a colon, as in $\{x \in A : p(x)\}$. |
| <a id="n2.5"></a>n2.5   | $\operatorname{card} A$;<br>$\vert A\vert$;<br>$\# A$ | the number of elements in $A$, the cardinality of $A$ |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| <a id="n2.6"></a>n2.6   | $\varnothing$                                         | the empty set                                 | $\emptyset$ should not be used.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| <a id="n2.7"></a>n2.7   | $B \subseteq A$                                       | $B$ is contained in $A$, $B$ is a subset of $A$ | Every element of $B$ belongs to $A$.<br>$\subset$ may also be used for this meaning, but see the note for [n2.8](#n2.8).<br>$A \supseteq B$ and $B \subseteq A$ are synonymous.                                                                                                                                                       |
| <a id="n2.8"></a>n2.8   | $B \subset A$                                         | $B$ is properly contained in $A$, $B$ is a proper subset of $A$ | Every element of $B$ belongs to $A$, and at least one element of $A$ does not belong to $B$.<br>If $\subset$ is taken to have the meaning of [n2.7](#n2.7), then the symbol for [n2.8](#n2.8) should be $\subsetneq$.<br>$A \supset B$ and $B \subset A$ are synonymous.                                        |
| <a id="n2.9"></a>n2.9   | $A \cup B$                                            | the union of $A$ and $B$                      | $A \cup B := \{x ~\vert~ x \in A \lor x \in B\}$;<br>for the definition of $:=$, see [n4.3](#n4.3)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| <a id="n2.10"></a>n2.10 | $A \cap B$                                            | the intersection of $A$ and $B$               | $A \cap B := \{x ~\vert~ x \in A \land x \in B\}$;<br>for the definition of $:=$, see [n4.3](#n4.3)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| <a id="n2.11"></a>n2.11 | $\displaystyle \bigcup\limits_{i=1}^n A_i$            | the union of the sets $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$  | $\displaystyle \bigcup\limits_{i=1}^n A_i=A_1\cup A_2\cup \dots \cup A_n$;<br>you may also use $\displaystyle \bigcup\nolimits_{i=1}^n$, $\displaystyle \bigcup\limits_{i\in I}$, $\displaystyle \bigcup\nolimits_{i\in I}$, where $I$ denotes the index set;<br>further, letting $P(i)$ be some proposition about $i$, $\displaystyle \bigcup_{P(i)} A_i$ denotes the union of the $A_i$ over all $i$ for which $P(i)$ is true |
| <a id="n2.12"></a>n2.12 | $\displaystyle \bigcap\limits_{i=1}^n A_i$            | the intersection of the sets $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ | $\displaystyle \bigcap\limits_{i=1}^n A_i=A_1\cap A_2\cap \dots \cap A_n$;<br>you may also use $\displaystyle \bigcap\nolimits_{i=1}^n$, $\displaystyle \bigcap\limits_{i\in I}$, $\displaystyle \bigcap\nolimits_{i\in I}$, where $I$ denotes the index set;<br>further, letting $P(i)$ be some proposition about $i$, $\displaystyle \bigcap_{P(i)} A_i$ denotes the intersection of the $A_i$ over all $i$ for which $P(i)$ is true |
| <a id="n2.13"></a>n2.13 | $A \setminus B$                                       | the difference of $A$ and $B$                 | $A \setminus B = \{x ~\vert~ x \in A \land x \notin B\}$;<br>$A - B$ should not be used;<br>when $B$ is a subset of $A$, $\complement_A B$ may also be used, and $A$ may be omitted if it is clear from context which set $A$ is being considered.<br>Where no ambiguity arises, $\overline{B}$ may also be used for the complement of the set $B$. |
| <a id="n2.14"></a>n2.14 | $(a, b)$                                              | the ordered pair $a$, $b$;<br>the ordered couple $a$, $b$ | $(a, b) = (c, d)$ if and only if $a = c$ and $b = d$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| <a id="n2.15"></a>n2.15 | $(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n)$                              | the ordered $n$-tuple                         | See [n2.14](#n2.14).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| <a id="n2.16"></a>n2.16 | $A \times B$                                          | the Cartesian product of the sets $A$ and $B$ | $A \times B = \{(x, y) ~\vert~ x \in A \land y \in B\}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| <a id="n2.17"></a>n2.17 | $\displaystyle \prod\limits_{i=1}^{n} A_i$            | the Cartesian product of the sets $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ | $\displaystyle \prod\limits_{i=1}^{n} A_i=\{(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) ~\vert~ x_1 \in A_1, x_2 \in A_2, \dots, x_n \in A_n\}$;<br>$A \times A \times \dots \times A$ is written $A^n$, where $n$ is the number of factors in the product;<br>for another usage of this symbol, see [n6.8](#n6.8) |
| <a id="n2.18"></a>n2.18 | $\mathrm{id}_A$                                       | the diagonal set of $A\times A$               | $\mathrm{id}_A=\{(x, x)~\vert~x\in A\}$;<br>if it is clear from context which set $A$ is being considered, $A$ may be omitted.                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| <a id="n2.19"></a>n2.19 | $\mathbf{1}_A$                                        | the indicator function                        | $\mathbf{1}_A(a)=[a\in A]$; for the definition of $[\cdot]$, see [n6.24](#n6.24).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| <a id="n2.20"></a>n2.20 | $\mathcal{P}(A)$;<br>$2^A$                            | the power set                                 | $\mathcal{P}(A)=\{S:S\subseteq A\}$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

## Standard number sets and intervals

| No.                     | Symbol, expression | Meaning, equivalent expression | Notes and examples                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a id="n3.1"></a>n3.1   | $\mathbf{N}$ | the set of natural numbers | $\mathbf{N} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\}$;<br>$\mathbf{N}^* = \mathbf{N}_+ = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$;<br>other restrictions may be added like so: $\mathbf{N}_{> 5} = \{n \in \mathbf{N} ~\vert~ n > 5\}$;<br>$\mathbb{N}$ may also be used. |
| <a id="n3.2"></a>n3.2   | $\mathbf{Z}$ | the set of integers | $\mathbf{Z}^* = \mathbf{Z}_+ = \{n \in \mathbf{Z} ~\vert~ n \ne 0\}$;<br>other restrictions may be added like so: $\mathbf{Z}_{> -3} = \{n \in \mathbf{Z} ~\vert~ n > -3\}$;<br>$\mathbb{Z}$ may also be used. |
| <a id="n3.3"></a>n3.3   | $\mathbf{Q}$ | the set of rational numbers | $\mathbf{Q}^* = \mathbf{Q}_+ = \{r \in \mathbf{Q} ~\vert~ r \ne 0\}$;<br>other restrictions may be added like so: $\mathbf{Q}_{< 0} = \{r \in \mathbf{Q} ~\vert~ r < 0\}$;<br>$\mathbb{Q}$ may also be used. |
| <a id="n3.4"></a>n3.4   | $\mathbf{R}$ | the set of real numbers | $\mathbf{R}^* = \mathbf{R}_+ = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ x \ne 0\}$;<br>other restrictions may be added like so: $\mathbf{R}_{> 0} = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ x > 0\}$;<br>$\mathbb{R}$ may also be used. |
| <a id="n3.5"></a>n3.5   | $\mathbf{C}$ | the set of complex numbers | $\mathbf{C}^* = \mathbf{C}_+ = \{z \in \mathbf{C} ~\vert~ z \ne 0\}$;<br>$\mathbb{C}$ may also be used. |
| <a id="n3.6"></a>n3.6   | $\mathbf{P}$ | the set of (positive) primes | $\mathbf{P} = \{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, \dots\}$;<br>$\mathbb{P}$ may also be used. |
| <a id="n3.7"></a>n3.7   | $[a, b]$     | the closed interval from $a$ to $b$ | $[a, b] = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ a \leq x \leq b\}$. |
| <a id="n3.8"></a>n3.8   | $(a, b]$     | the left-open, right-closed interval from $a$ to $b$ | $(a, b] = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ a < x \leq b\}$;<br>$(-\infty, b] = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ x \leq b\}$. |
| <a id="n3.9"></a>n3.9   | $[a, b)$     | the left-closed, right-open interval from $a$ to $b$ | $[a, b) = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ a \leq x < b\}$;<br>$[a, +\infty) = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ a \leq x\}$. |
| <a id="n3.10"></a>n3.10 | $(a, b)$     | the open interval from $a$ to $b$ | $(a, b) = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ a < x < b\}$;<br>$(-\infty, b) = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ x < b\}$;<br>$(a, +\infty) = \{x \in \mathbf{R} ~\vert~ a < x\}$. |

## Relations

| No.                     | Symbol, expression   | Meaning, equivalent expression | Notes and examples                                                                                            |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a id="n4.1"></a>n4.1   | $a = b$              | $a$ equals $b$      | $\equiv$ is used to emphasize that an equation is an identity.<br>For another meaning of this symbol, see [n4.18](#n4.18). |
| <a id="n4.2"></a>n4.2   | $a \ne b$            | $a$ is not equal to $b$ |                                                                                                          |
| <a id="n4.3"></a>n4.3   | $a := b$             | $a$ is defined as $b$ | See [n2.9](#n2.9), [n2.10](#n2.10)                                                                          |
| <a id="n4.4"></a>n4.4   | $a \approx b$        | $a$ is approximately equal to $b$ | Equality is not excluded.                                                                                |
| <a id="n4.5"></a>n4.5   | $a \simeq b$         | $a$ is asymptotically equal to $b$ | For example:<br>as $x\to a$, $\dfrac{1}{\sin(x-a)} \simeq \dfrac{1}{x-a}$;<br>for the meaning of $x \to a$, see [n4.15](#n4.15). |
| <a id="n4.6"></a>n4.6   | $a \propto b$        | $a$ is proportional to $b$ | $a \sim b$ may also be used.<br>$\sim$ is also used to denote an equivalence relation.                       |
| <a id="n4.7"></a>n4.7   | $M \cong N$          | $M$ is congruent to $N$ | When $M$ and $N$ are point sets (geometric figures).<br>This symbol is also used to denote the isomorphism of algebraic structures. |
| <a id="n4.8"></a>n4.8   | $a < b$              | $a$ is less than $b$ |                                                                                                             |
| <a id="n4.9"></a>n4.9   | $b > a$              | $b$ is greater than $a$ |                                                                                                          |
| <a id="n4.10"></a>n4.10 | $a \leq b$           | $a$ is less than or equal to $b$ |                                                                                                     |
| <a id="n4.11"></a>n4.11 | $b \geq a$           | $b$ is greater than or equal to $a$ |                                                                                                  |
| <a id="n4.12"></a>n4.12 | $a \ll b$            | $a$ is much less than $b$ |                                                                                                        |
| <a id="n4.13"></a>n4.13 | $b \gg a$            | $b$ is much greater than $a$ |                                                                                                     |
| <a id="n4.14"></a>n4.14 | $\infty$             | infinity | This symbol is **not** a number.<br>$+\infty$, $-\infty$ may also be used.                                          |
| <a id="n4.15"></a>n4.15 | $x \to a$            | $x$ tends to $a$ | Usually appears in limit expressions.<br>$a$ may also be $\infty$, $+\infty$, $-\infty$.                             |
| <a id="n4.16"></a>n4.16 | $m \mid n$           | $m$ divides $n$ | For integers $m$, $n$:<br>$(\exists~k \in \mathbf{Z})~~m\cdot k = n$.                                                 |
| <a id="n4.17"></a>n4.17 | $m \perp n$          | $m$ is coprime to $n$ | For integers $m$, $n$:<br>$(\nexists~k \in \mathbf{Z}_{>1})~~(k \mid m) \land (k \mid n)$;<br>for another usage of this symbol, see [n5.2](#n5.2) |
| <a id="n4.18"></a>n4.18 | $n \equiv k \pmod m$ | $n$ is congruent to $k$ modulo $m$ | For integers $n$, $k$, $m$:<br>$m \mid (n - k)$;<br>do not confuse this with the meaning mentioned in [n4.1](#n4.1). |

## Scalars and vectors

In this section, the basis vectors are denoted $\boldsymbol{e}_1$, $\boldsymbol{e}_2$, $\boldsymbol{e}_3$. Many of the concepts in this section can be generalized to $n$-dimensional space.

Scalars and vectors themselves are independent of the choice of coordinate system, whereas each scalar component of a vector depends on the choice of coordinate system.

For basis vectors $\boldsymbol{e}_1$, $\boldsymbol{e}_2$, $\boldsymbol{e}_3$, every vector $\boldsymbol{a}$ can be written as $\boldsymbol{a}=a_1\boldsymbol{e}_1+a_2\boldsymbol{e}_2+a_3\boldsymbol{e}_3$, where $a_1$, $a_2$, and $a_3$ are uniquely determined scalar values, called the "coordinates" of the vector with respect to that basis; $a_1\boldsymbol{e}_1$, $a_2\boldsymbol{e}_2$, and $a_3\boldsymbol{e}_3$ are called the component vectors of the vector with respect to that basis.

In this section, only Cartesian (orthogonal) coordinates of ordinary space are considered. Cartesian coordinates are denoted $x$, $y$, $z$ or $a_1$, $a_2$, $a_3$ or $x_1$, $x_2$, $x_3$.

Throughout this section, all subscripts $i$, $j$, $k$ range from $1$ to $3$.

## Special functions

In this section, $z$, $w$ are complex numbers, $k$, $n$ are natural numbers, and $k\leq n$.
