This page mainly lists some resources related to competitive programming, such as online judges, problem collections, books, and tools.

## Online judges

Online judges (Online Judging Systems, OJs) are generally used for problem-solving practice, participating in and organizing contests, and communication and sharing among users.

### Domestic

-   [51Nod](https://www.51nod.com/): Has many math problems and thinking problems worth trying.
-   [Comet OJ](https://www.cometoj.com): Started in 2018, aims to provide a platform for competition, practice, and communication for algorithm enthusiasts; it often holds original high-quality contests and has a rich problem bank.
-   [HDU Online Judge](http://acm.hdu.edu.cn/): Started in 2005, the online judge of Hangzhou Dianzi University, with multi-university training problems.
-   [HydroOJ](https://hydro.ac/): Started in 2021, the official site of the open-source project [Hydro](https://hydro.js.org/). Users can create their own [domains](https://hydro.ac/discuss/6087cc44e098b0cd7dde1a0c), in which features available on the main site such as the problem bank, contests, and discussions can be used.
-   [Judge Duck Online](https://duck.ac/): Based on the open-source project [JudgeDuck](https://github.com/JudgeDuck) developed by [松松松](https://github.com/wangyisong1996); it can measure the running time of a judged program to the microsecond. (Few problems.)
-   [LibreOJ](https://loj.ac/): Started in 2017. Based on the open-source project [Lyrio](https://github.com/lyrio-dev/lyrio); "Libre" means "free". All test data of the problems and the submitted code are open to all users. Currently maintained by [Menci](https://github.com/Menci).
-   [CDOJ](https://cdoj.site/d/lutece/): The online judge of the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, started in 2012.
-   [Luogu](https://www.luogu.com.cn/): Started in 2013, with a huge community; its collection of past problems and exercises for all kinds of OI is relatively complete. It provides paid educational services.
-   [Nowcoder](https://www.nowcoder.com/): Started in 2014, providing services such as technical job-hunting preparation, community exchange, and corporate recruiting.
-   [OpenJudge](http://openjudge.cn/): Started in 2005, a group-judging platform developed by the POJ team.
-   [POJ](http://poj.org/): The online judge of Peking University, started in 2003, one of the oldest OJs in China. It has many English problems, both basic ones and good ones worth a try. POJ's problems can be submitted in the [Bailian](http://bailian.openjudge.cn/practice/) problem bank.
-   [PTA (PinTia)](https://pintia.cn/): Started in 2016, a product of Hangzhou Baiteng Education Technology Co., Ltd., a spin-off of Zhejiang University.
-   [QOJ](https://qoj.ac/): Collects many domestic and foreign OI and ICPC competition problems, with training value.
-   [Universal Online Judge](https://uoj.ac/): Started in 2014; "Universal" means "general-purpose", the [project is open source](https://github.com/UniversalOJ/UOJ-System); the OJ of [VFK](https://github.com/vfleaking): many original contest problems and CCF/THU problems, of relatively high difficulty.
-   [Vijos](https://vijos.org/): Started in 2005. Projects such as the [server](https://github.com/vijos/vj4) and the [judge](https://github.com/vijos/jd4) are open source.
-   [WZOI](https://wzoi.cn): Started in 2017, an [open-source](https://github.com/massimodong/wzoj) judge maintained by Wenzhou High School in Zhejiang Province.
-   [ZOJ](https://zoj.pintia.cn/home): The online judge of Zhejiang University, started in 2001.

### Foreign

-   [AizuOJ](https://onlinejudge.u-aizu.ac.jp): The online judge of the University of Aizu, Japan, started in 2004. It contains problems from several Japanese high-school and university programming contests, and comes with introductory courses on programming/data structures/algorithms.
-   [AtCoder](https://atcoder.jp/): A Japanese OJ; the Japanese version has contests of Japanese schools that are not shown in the English version. The problems are interesting and of relatively high quality.
-   [CodeChef](https://codechef.com/): An Indian OJ that holds contests periodically. The system is based on SPOJ's Sphere Engine.
-   [Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/): A Russian OJ, started in 2010; its founder is [Mike Mirzayanov](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mike-mirzayanov-31772a93/). It has many series of contests and supports individual problem-setting and applying to organize contests. The problems are of relatively high quality.
-   [CSES](https://cses.fi/problemset/) (Code Submission Evaluation System): a topic-divided problem bank, [aiming](https://cses.fi/problemset/text/2433) to become a comprehensive high-quality problem bank, mainly developed by Antti Laaksonen, author of the [Competitive Programmer's Handbook](https://cses.fi/book/book.pdf); started in 2013.
-   [CS Academy](https://csacademy.com/)
-   [DMOJ](https://dmoj.ca/): A Canadian open-source OJ with broad language support; the problem bank is an archive of major contests, and it also periodically holds its own contests.
-   [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/): Has many contests.
-   [Kattis](https://open.kattis.com/): The problem bank mainly contains ICPC-like contest problems; it rates users according to their problem-solving and recommends problems of four difficulty classes—trivial/easy/medium/hard—suitable for the user's level, where the problem difficulty is assessed using an [ELO-rating](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%AD%89%E7%BA%A7%E5%88%86)-like system.
-   [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/): A coding-interview problem-practice site, with a Chinese sub-site: [LeetCode China](https://leetcode.cn).
-   [Light OJ](https://lightoj.com)
-   [Open Trains](https://opentrains.opencup.org/): The training platform of Russia's Open Cup contest, built on the open-source [ejudge](https://ejudge.ru/) system, supporting virtual contests; the problem bank contains past Open Cup problems and problems from the Petrozavodsk camp.
-   [SPOJ](http://www.spoj.com): Started in 2003; its backend system [Sphere Engine](https://sphere-engine.com/) was commercialized in 2008; it supports problem upvoting and tagging.
-   [TopCoder](https://www.topcoder.com/): Started in 2001; its [competitive-programming community](https://www.topcoder.com/community/competitive-programming/) has many contests; its current main business is technology crowdsourcing.
-   [TimusOJ](http://acm.timus.ru/): Started in 2000, developed by Ural Federal University, with the largest online judge problem bank in Russia; the problems mainly come from Ural Federal University's school contests, the Ural Championship, the ICPC Ural regional contest, and the Petrozavodsk camp.
-   Online Judge (formerly [UVaOJ](https://uva.onlinejudge.org/)): Started in 1995, the earliest internationally famous OJ; its founder was Professor Miguel Ángel Revilla of the University of Valladolid (UVa) in Spain; because [Professor Revilla passed away in 2018](https://www.elnortedecastilla.es/valladolid/muere-profesor-miguel-20180402225739-nt.html) and the University of Valladolid ceased maintenance, UVaOJ was renamed Online Judge from July 2019. The current maintainer of this platform is [building a new judging platform on GitHub](https://github.com/TheOnlineJudge/ojudge).
-   [Yandex](https://contest.yandex.ru/): Has archived the All-Russian Olympiad in Informatics of recent years.

## Tools

-   [*100 gdb Tips*](https://github.com/hellogcc/100-gdb-tips)
-   [Algorithm Visualizer](http://algorithm-visualizer.org)
-   [cppreference](https://zh.cppreference.com/w/): a comprehensive online reference for the C and C++ languages and their standard libraries
-   [Compiler Explorer](https://godbolt.org): view online the assembly statements corresponding to compiled code blocks, supporting the choice of different compilers
-   [C++ Insights](https://cppinsights.io/): view your C++ source code from the compiler's perspective
-   [Inverse Symbolic Calculator](http://wayback.cecm.sfu.ca/projects/ISC/ISCmain.html): look up an expression from a real number, useful for reverse-engineering constants
-   [$\rm\LaTeX$ handwritten symbol recognition](http://detexify.kirelabs.org/classify.html)
-   [$\rm\LaTeX$ math formula reference](http://www.mohu.org/info/symbols/symbols.htm)
-   [Mathpix](https://mathpix.com/): convert a screenshot to $\rm\LaTeX{}$
-   [OEIS](https://oeis.org): an integer-sequence search engine
-   [Python Tutor](https://pythontutor.com/): visualization of the code-execution process
-   [Quick C++ Benchmark](https://quick-bench.com/): compare the running speed of two or more functions online
-   [Try It Online](https://tio.run): run code in 600+ languages online, supporting IO interaction, with a 60s timeout and code sharing
-   [Graph editor](https://csacademy.com/app/graph_editor/) and [GraphViz](http://www.graphviz.org/)
-   [uDebug](https://www.udebug.com): provides debugging aids for some OJ problems
-   [USF](https://www.cs.usfca.edu/~galles/visualization/) and [VisuAlgo](https://visualgo.net/zh): algorithm visualization
-   [Wandbox](https://wandbox.org/): online code running, supporting 30+ languages, with code sharing and support for different compiler versions
-   [Wolfram Alpha](https://www.wolframalpha.com/): can compute problems on many topics including mathematics, science and technology, society and culture, and so on
